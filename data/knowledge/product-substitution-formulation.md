# Product Substitution and Reformulation Away from ING-PEC-450

This document sets out Lactovia's technical position on reformulating chilled dairy products away from high-methoxyl pectin **ING-PEC-450 ("High-Methoxyl Pectin HM-450")**, supplied single-source by SUP-EU-014 Valencia Pectinas S.A. (Valencia, Spain), which declared force majeure on 2026-09-03. Fourteen SKUs across France, Germany and Belgium are affected, with plant depletion at 12 days (PLANT-FR-01 Rennes), 15 days (PLANT-DE-02 Bremen) and 18 days (PLANT-BE-01 Ghent).

## What HM pectin actually does in these products

ING-PEC-450 is a high-methoxyl pectin, additive **E440(i)**. It gels through hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interaction — not through calcium — and requires two conditions simultaneously: **pH below 3.5** and **soluble solids above 55 % Brix**. Both conditions are met in Lactovia's fruit preparations, which sit at pH 3.2–3.4 and 60–64 % Brix.

In the finished product HM pectin delivers three things:

1. **The fruit-prep set** — a firm, thermally irreversible gel that holds fruit pieces in suspension through the 14-day chilled shelf life instead of letting them sediment to the cup base.
2. **Syneresis control** — it binds free water and prevents the watery weep at the cup surface that drives the single largest consumer complaint category in chilled yogurt.
3. **The clean short bite** — a brittle, short texture that fractures cleanly on the spoon. This is the specific mouthfeel consumers associate with the **Lactovia Gourmet** range, and it is the hardest attribute to replicate with any alternative.

Typical usage rate is **0.6 %** on fruit-prep mass.

## Alternative 1 — ING-PEC-220 Low-Methoxyl Pectin LM-220

LM pectin gels by a completely different mechanism: it is **calcium-dependent**, forming egg-box junction zones with divalent cations, and it works at **lower soluble solids** (it will set below 40 % Brix, where HM pectin will not set at all).

Consequences for Lactovia:

- Requires a **calcium salt addition** (calcium citrate or dicalcium phosphate) dosed into the fruit prep, which is a new raw material, a new dosing station and a new HACCP control point.
- Requires a **full fruit-prep recipe rebuild** — Brix, acid and calcium must be re-balanced together, not adjusted independently.
- **Usage rate rises from 0.6 % to 0.9 %**, a 50 % increase in hydrocolloid mass, with a corresponding cost and viscosity impact upstream of the filler.
- Delivers a **softer, more elastic set** — the gel yields rather than fractures. It is a competent texture but it is not the Gourmet short bite.

**Critical regulatory constraint:** the amidated form of low-methoxyl pectin, **E440(ii)**, is **not permitted in EU organic production under Regulation (EU) 2018/848**. Several Lactovia Naturel and Lactovia Kids SKUs carry EU organic certification. Those SKUs **cannot move to amidated LM pectin at all** — the only LM route open to them is conventional (non-amidated) LM-220, which is more calcium-sensitive and gives a narrower processing window.

## Alternative 2 — ING-GEL-110 Bovine Gelatin 220 Bloom

Gelatin gives the best mouthfeel of any alternative: a smooth melt-in-mouth release that sensory panels rate highly on creaminess and richness. It is also the alternative Lactovia is least able to use.

- It is **bovine-derived**. It destroys **vegetarian**, **Halal** and **Kosher** claims outright. Several affected SKUs carry vegetarian claims on front-of-pack.
- It triggers a **mandatory label change** — the ingredient declaration must state the animal origin. There is no derogation that allows an undeclared species change.
- It **melts above 35 °C**. Any product that leaves the chilled chain — including in-store ambient promotional display, which several German retail customers operate — loses its structure irreversibly.
- It is **unacceptable for the Lactovia Naturel range**, whose entire commercial positioning is clean-label and short-ingredient-list. Adding gelatin to Naturel contradicts the brand proposition.

Gelatin is therefore viable only for a narrow subset of conventional, non-claim-bearing, strictly chilled SKUs.

## Alternative 3 — ING-STA-330 Modified Waxy Maize Starch

Starch is the fastest and cheapest option to source — multiple qualified suppliers, no single-source exposure, and lead times measured in days rather than weeks.

It is also the weakest technically:

- Delivers a **pasty, cohesive texture**. Sensory panels **reliably down-rate** it against an HM pectin control; this is a consistent, reproducible finding across Lactovia's panel history, not a marginal effect.
- Adds a detectable **starchy note** in the flavour profile, most noticeable in low-acid and delicate fruit variants (pear, vanilla, apricot).
- Requires a **"modified starch" declaration** on the ingredient list, which **conflicts directly with clean-label claims** on the Naturel range.

## Blends — the pragmatic compromise

The realistic technical answer for most affected SKUs is a **blend of LM pectin ING-PEC-220 and modified starch ING-STA-330**. The LM pectin supplies the calcium-set structure and the water binding; the starch supplies bulk viscosity at lower LM inclusion, holding total hydrocolloid cost near parity. Blends recover roughly 70–80 % of the HM pectin texture on panel scoring — enough to pass on mid-tier SKUs, generally not enough to pass on flagship Gourmet SKUs.

## Re-validation pathway and critical path

Every reformulation must complete the following sequence. The steps are largely serial because each stage gates the next.

| Stage | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bench trial | 5 days | Lab-scale gel formation, Brix/pH/calcium matrix |
| Pilot plant | 3 days | Scale-up, shear and pumping behaviour |
| Sensory triangle test, 40 panellists | 7 days | Acceptance: **no significant difference at p<0.05**, or hedonic score **within 0.5 points of control on a 9-point scale** |
| Accelerated shelf-life | 14 days | Syneresis, set firmness, colour |
| Real-time shelf-life confirmation | 28 days | Runs in parallel from day 1 of accelerated |
| Industrial validation run | 2 days | Full-line trial, filler and sealer behaviour |

Realistic critical path is **31–45 days**. It **cannot be compressed below approximately 28 days** — the 28-day real-time shelf-life confirmation is a hard floor for a 14-day chilled product, and no amount of resourcing shortens elapsed calendar time on a shelf-life study.

## The 2024 precedent

In 2024 Lactovia attempted to move **three Kids SKUs** from HM to LM pectin. **Only one passed sensory.** The programme is recorded with an effectiveness score of **44 out of 100**. The two failures were both fruit-piece variants where the softer LM set allowed fruit sedimentation before end of shelf life. This precedent is the single strongest internal argument against treating substitution as a rapid mitigation.

## Labelling consequences

Any change to the hydrocolloid system changes the ingredient declaration. Two routes exist:

- **Full artwork change with new print plates: 6–8 weeks.** This is the compliant steady-state route.
- **Approved over-sticker derogation: valid for 90 days.** This permits interim supply while new artwork is produced, but it is time-boxed and must be closed out before expiry.

The 90-day over-sticker window is longer than the 45-day worst-case reformulation path, so labelling is not the binding constraint — validation is.

## Recommendation

**Substitution is a second-wave or structural mitigation. It must never be the sole answer to a 12-day depletion horizon at PLANT-FR-01.** The minimum 28-day validation floor exceeds every plant's current cover. Reformulation should be initiated immediately in parallel, because it is the durable answer to single-source exposure on SUP-EU-014 — but the 12-, 15- and 18-day gaps must be closed by alternate sourcing, production rescheduling and de-campaigning, not by formulation change.
