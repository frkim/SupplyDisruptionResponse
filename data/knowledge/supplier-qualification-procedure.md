# Lactovia Supplier Qualification Procedure — Food-Grade Ingredient Suppliers

## Purpose and scope

This procedure governs how Lactovia qualifies a new supplier of a food-grade ingredient for commercial supply into any of its five plants: PLANT-FR-01 (Rennes), PLANT-DE-02 (Bremen), PLANT-BE-01 (Ghent), PLANT-FR-03 (Lyon) and PLANT-ES-01 (Zaragoza). It applies to all direct ingredient categories including hydrocolloids, dairy inputs, cultures, sweeteners, flavourings and primary packaging in food contact.

No supplier may ship commercial volume of an ingredient into a Lactovia plant until it holds a status of **qualified** or **conditionally qualified** for that specific ingredient. Qualification is granted per supplier *and* per ingredient code — a supplier qualified for ING-PEC-220 is not thereby qualified for ING-PEC-450.

## The four stage gates

Qualification runs through four sequential gates. Each has a single accountable owner.

**G1 — Commercial and technical screening (owner: Procurement).** Procurement establishes that the supplier can plausibly serve the requirement: nameplate capacity against Lactovia's forecast volume, indicative pricing in EUR, lead time, incoterms, geographic and logistics feasibility, and a financial stability check drawn from audited accounts for the last two fiscal years plus a third-party credit rating. Technical fit is screened at a high level — does the supplier's product grade match the functional specification. G1 typically takes 1–2 weeks and terminates roughly a third of candidates.

**G2 — Documentation and specification review (owner: Quality & Regulatory).** The supplier submits a full documentation pack. Quality & Regulatory reviews it against the Lactovia ingredient specification and rejects on any material gap. G2 typically takes 2–3 weeks, extending when certificates of analysis must be generated on new production lots.

**G3 — On-site audit (owner: Quality & Regulatory, with Manufacturing participation).** A physical audit of the manufacturing site. G3 typically takes 1–2 weeks of elapsed time once scheduled, but scheduling and travel commonly add 2–4 weeks for non-European sites.

**G4 — Industrial validation trial and release (owner: Manufacturing, with Quality sign-off).** A full-scale production trial at the receiving plant using supplier material, followed by finished-product testing, shelf-life confirmation and sensory panel where the ingredient is functionally or organoleptically active. G4 typically takes 2–3 weeks. Release is granted by Quality on Manufacturing's trial report.

## Timeline

Elapsed time from G1 start to G4 release is normally **6 to 10 weeks** depending on the supplier's document readiness, geography and whether organic scope is in play.

| Week | Activity | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | G1 screening: capacity, pricing, lead time, financial stability check | Procurement |
| 2 | G1 close; documentation pack requested | Procurement |
| 3 | G2 review: certification, HACCP, allergen declaration | Quality & Regulatory |
| 4 | G2 review: certificates of analysis on three lots, traceability file | Quality & Regulatory |
| 5 | G2 close; audit scheduled and travel booked | Quality & Regulatory |
| 6 | G3 on-site audit executed | Quality & Regulatory / Manufacturing |
| 7 | G3 findings issued; corrective action plan agreed | Quality & Regulatory |
| 8 | G4 industrial trial at receiving plant | Manufacturing |
| 9 | G4 finished-product testing, shelf-life, sensory panel | Manufacturing / Quality |
| 10 | Release decision and status assignment | Quality & Regulatory |

For a European supplier with a current certification file and no organic scope, this compresses to 6 weeks. For an Asian or Latin American supplier requiring travel, translation of documentation and a first-time audit, 10 weeks is realistic and 12 is not unusual.

## Documentation requirements at G2

The G2 pack must contain, at minimum:

- A valid **FSSC 22000 or ISO 22000** certificate covering the manufacturing site and the product category, in date at the time of review.
- A **HACCP plan** for the supplying line, with hazard analysis and identified critical control points.
- A full **allergen declaration** covering the fourteen EU-declarable allergens, including cross-contact risk on shared lines and the cleaning validation that supports any "may contain" position.
- **Pesticide-residue and heavy-metal certificates of analysis on three consecutive production lots.** Single-lot data is not accepted. For plant-derived ingredients such as pectin, the pesticide panel must reflect the feedstock crop's typical treatment regime.
- **Traceability to feedstock origin** — the supplier must be able to trace a finished lot back to the agricultural or dairy source, with documented one-up/one-down records.
- A **financial stability check** confirming the supplier is a going concern capable of sustaining committed volume.
- For any ingredient destined for an organic-labelled line, a valid **EU organic operator certificate under Regulation (EU) 2018/848**, covering both the site and the specific product.

## On-site audit scope at G3

The audit covers good manufacturing practice, plant hygiene and pest control, raw material inbound controls, process control and CCP monitoring records, cleaning and allergen changeover validation, laboratory competence and method validation, traceability by lot reconstruction, complaint and recall handling, and where relevant organic segregation controls.

Audits close with findings graded critical, major or minor. Critical findings block qualification outright. Major findings require an agreed corrective action plan with evidence of closure before release.

An **unannounced follow-up audit is required within 12 months** of any initial qualification audit. Failure to permit the unannounced follow-up reverts the supplier to *audit_required* status and suspends commercial supply.

## Status definitions

**Qualified.** All four gates passed, no open major findings, documentation current. The supplier may ship commercial volume of the specified ingredient into any Lactovia plant and onto any line, including organic and Kids-branded products.

**Conditionally qualified.** G1 and G2 passed; G3 not yet closed, either because the audit has not been executed or because major findings remain open with an agreed corrective action plan. Commercial supply is permitted **only on non-organic lines and only on non-Kids-branded products**, pending audit closure. Volume is capped at the level stated in the conditional release. Conditional status is time-boxed and reviewed quarterly.

**Audit_required.** The supplier has not been audited by Lactovia for this ingredient, or a prior audit has lapsed or produced unresolved critical findings. No commercial supply is permitted. The full G1–G4 path applies.

## Emergency derogation path

Under a **documented force-majeure event** affecting an existing source, the timeline may be compressed to **3–4 weeks** by running G2 and G3 in parallel, accepting a remote or hybrid audit for the initial pass, and reducing the G4 trial to a single validation batch with accelerated release testing.

Approval requires **both the Quality Director and a named executive sponsor** — typically the Chief Supply Chain Officer or the Chief Operating Officer — recorded against the specific incident reference. Procurement cannot self-authorise.

Constraints are absolute and non-negotiable:

- **No organic-labelled SKUs.** Regulation (EU) 2018/848 conformity cannot be established on a compressed file.
- **No infant products and no Lactovia Kids-branded lines.**
- **Maximum 90-day validity** from the release date. The derogation expires automatically.
- **Mandatory retrospective full qualification** — the complete G1–G4 path, including a physical on-site audit, must be executed within the 90-day window or supply stops.

## Application to the current pectin situation

For the ING-PEC-450 disruption declared by SUP-EU-014 on 2026-09-03, the three alternate pectin sources sit in materially different positions.

**SUP-EU-023, Rheinland Hydrocolloids GmbH (Krefeld, Germany)** is already **qualified** for ING-PEC-450 and requires no gate work. Supply can be placed immediately, subject only to its commercial constraints: roughly 18 percent price premium, 21-day lead time, and a capacity ceiling of about 40,000 kg per month. Qualification is not the bottleneck here — capacity is.

**SUP-LATAM-004, Citrus Andina Ltda (Curitiba, Brazil)** is **conditionally qualified**. G1 and G2 are complete; G3 must be closed. Estimated **4 weeks plus travel** to audit execution and findings closure. Until G3 closes, Citrus Andina material may only be used on non-organic, non-Kids lines. Its 55-day lead time means qualification closure and first delivery are roughly concurrent, so G3 work should start immediately if this source is to be usable at all.

**SUP-APAC-009, Nanjing Botanical Extracts (Nanjing, China)** is **audit_required** and needs the full G1–G4 path, estimated at **10 weeks**. It offers the lowest price but has a 60-day lead time and unresolved EU organic certification concerns, and therefore **cannot serve organic-labelled SKUs** under any status short of a complete organic operator certificate under Regulation (EU) 2018/848. It is not a viable first-wave response to a 12-to-18-day depletion horizon.

## Common causes of qualification failure

The most frequent G2 failure is **incomplete certificate-of-analysis coverage** — suppliers routinely submit a single favourable lot rather than three consecutive production lots, which masks lot-to-lot variability and is rejected on sight.

The second most frequent is **traceability that stops at the supplier's own goods-in**, with no documented link to the agricultural feedstock. For plant-derived hydrocolloids this is disqualifying, because it makes a feedstock-level contamination event — precisely the failure mode seen in 2024 — untraceable and uncontainable.

At G3, the recurring failures are **inadequate allergen changeover validation on shared lines** and **weak laboratory method validation**, where the supplier's internal test methods have not been demonstrated fit for the specification limits they are certifying against.

At G4, most failures are **sensory or functional**, not safety-related: the material meets specification on paper but performs differently in the finished product — texture, set behaviour, syneresis or mouthfeel drift outside the finished-product tolerance. This is why G4 cannot be skipped for functionally active ingredients such as pectin, and why the emergency derogation path still requires at least one validation batch.

Finally, a material minority of candidates fail at **G1 on financial stability or capacity**, which is why that screening sits first and cheapest in the sequence.
