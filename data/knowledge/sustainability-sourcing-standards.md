# Lactovia Sustainability and Responsible Sourcing Standards

Owner: Sustainability Direction, Lactovia Group, Rennes. Version 3.1, effective 2026-01-01. Binding on all ingredient sourcing decisions, including emergency and single-shipment purchases.

## The commitment and why ingredients dominate it

Lactovia has committed to reduce Scope 3 purchased-goods-and-services emissions by 30% against a 2022 baseline by 2030, with an interim milestone of 12% by the end of 2026. Attainment at 2026-09-03 is 9.4%. The gap of 2.6 percentage points must be closed in the remaining four months of the year.

Scope 3 represents approximately 87% of the group footprint; Scopes 1 and 2 together are the residual 13%. Ingredient sourcing therefore dominates the group's climate performance, and a single emergency procurement decision can move the annual number more than a year of energy-efficiency work at the five plants. The annual ingredient footprint is approximately 74,000 tonnes CO2e.

## Supplier sustainability score

Every supplier is scored 0–100 on five weighted components:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Carbon intensity (cradle-to-Lactovia-gate, kg CO2e per kg delivered) | 30% |
| Water stewardship | 20% |
| Certifications and third-party audit | 20% |
| Labour and human-rights due diligence | 20% |
| Packaging and waste | 10% |

Thresholds: 70 and above is **preferred** and may be sourced without further approval. 50–69 is **acceptable with a documented improvement plan** and a review at 12 months. Below 50 requires **executive derogation** signed by the Sustainability Director and the CEO, time-limited, and reported to the Board.

## Air freight policy

Air freight for ingredients is prohibited as a matter of standard practice. Air transport raises transport-stage emissions by roughly a factor of 30 versus deep-sea container shipping, and no ingredient cost saving recovers that. Air freight is permitted only under a documented supply emergency, subject to all of the following: a written emergency declaration referencing the disrupted material; a hard cap of 90 days from first shipment; joint approval by the Sustainability Director and the CFO; mandatory purchase of offsets for the full incremental tonnage; and explicit disclosure of the incremental emissions in the annual sustainability statement. Air freight cannot be authorised by Procurement alone at any value.

## The arithmetic that decides this

Switching 180 tonnes from SUP-EU-014 at 1.9 kg CO2e/kg to SUP-APAC-009 at 5.6 adds approximately **666 tonnes CO2e**, equal to about **0.9% of the annual ingredient footprint**. That is absorbable, though it consumes roughly a third of a percentage point of the reduction trajectory and would need to be recovered elsewhere.

Doing the same volume **by air** is categorically different. Multiplying the transport stage by 30 raises the delivered intensity to roughly 113 kg CO2e/kg, giving on the order of **20,300 tonnes CO2e** for 180 tonnes of pectin — about 27% of the entire annual ingredient footprint and roughly 30 times the sea-freight penalty. A single air-freighted pectin programme would eliminate the 2026 milestone outright and reverse several years of accumulated reduction. Air freight from Brazil or China cuts lead time to 6–9 days; that speed is real, and it is not worth 20,000 tonnes.

Applied against the group internal carbon shadow price of EUR 100 per tonne CO2e, SUP-APAC-009 saves EUR 748,800 against SUP-EU-023 while adding 630 tonnes CO2e — an implied EUR 1,189 per tonne. The saving does not justify the emission at any plausible carbon price.

## CSRD and ESRS E1 consequences

Lactovia reports under CSRD. A material year-on-year increase in Scope 3 category 1 emissions must be identified, quantified and explained in the sustainability statement under ESRS E1, together with the mitigating actions and the revised trajectory to 2030. It cannot be absorbed quietly or netted against unrelated reductions. Emergency sourcing decisions that materially move Scope 3 must be logged at the time of the decision, with the tonnage and the rationale, so that the disclosure can be assembled without reconstruction.

## Human rights, deforestation and traceability

Any new pectin supplier must provide geolocation data for the citrus feedstock origin. Citrus peel is in scope for EU Deforestation Regulation due diligence where sourced from at-risk geographies, and Brazilian feedstock in particular requires plot-level coordinates and a legality statement before the first container is accepted. Labour and human-rights due diligence under CSDDD applies to both SUP-LATAM-004 and SUP-APAC-009; the Nanjing audit is the binding constraint on that source and cannot be waived for urgency.

## Customer-facing exposure

Several key accounts now embed supplier-emissions clauses in their supply agreements. CUST-004 REWE Group and CUST-007 Delhaize Belgium require annual reported improvement in the carbon intensity of supplied goods, and CUST-011 Colruyt Group requires prior notification of any material change in ingredient origin under its private-label specification. A high-carbon emergency source is therefore a commercial problem as well as an environmental one, and can trigger specification-change review at exactly the moment supply is least stable.

## Recommendation hierarchy for this incident

1. **SUP-EU-023 (Krefeld)** — preferred. Fastest at 21 days, lowest-carbon alternative at +36 tonnes CO2e, score 81, already qualified. The 18% premium (EUR 316,800 on 180 tonnes) is the correct price to pay.
2. **SUP-LATAM-004 (Curitiba), by sea** — acceptable as a structural second source to remove single-source dependency, conditional on a documented improvement plan against its score of 58 and EUDR geolocation evidence. Order now for 55-day arrival; do not rely on it for the 12–18 day depletion window.
3. **SUP-APAC-009 (Nanjing)** — last resort. Below the derogation threshold, audit outstanding, +666 tonnes CO2e.
4. **Air freight from Brazil or China** — requires explicit executive derogation, full offset of the incremental tonnage, 90-day cap and annual-report disclosure. The default answer is no.
