# EU Food Additive Regulatory Guide — Pectin (E440) and Dairy Reformulation at Lactovia

This guide is the working regulatory reference for Lactovia Group (HQ Rennes, France) when an ingredient substitution is proposed for yogurt, chilled dessert and fresh-milk lines produced at PLANT-FR-01 (Rennes), PLANT-DE-02 (Bremen), PLANT-BE-01 (Ghent), PLANT-FR-03 (Lyon) and PLANT-ES-01 (Zaragoza). It is written against the reference incident of 2026-09-03, in which single-source supplier SUP-EU-014 "Valencia Pectinas S.A." (Valencia, Spain) declared force majeure on ING-PEC-450 "High-Methoxyl Pectin HM-450", affecting 14 SKUs across France, Germany and Belgium.

## The Union list: Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008

Food additives placed on the EU market are governed by Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008. Only additives entered in the Union list in Annex II may be used, and only in the food categories, and at the maximum levels, specified there. An additive that is lawful in one category is not automatically lawful in another; the category number is the controlling fact, not the additive number.

Pectin is authorised as **E440**, subdivided into **E440(i) pectin** and **E440(ii) amidated pectin**. In the fermented milk products, cream products and edible-ice/dessert categories that cover essentially all Lactovia output, both forms carry **quantum satis** authorisation, meaning no numerical maximum applies and the use level is limited only by good manufacturing practice. Practically, Lactovia formulations sit between 0.3% and 1.2% pectin by finished-product weight. The absence of a numerical ceiling means a like-for-like pectin substitution is rarely blocked by Annex II itself — the constraints that bite are purity, labelling and organic rules.

Two carve-outs matter. First, additives including E440 are **not permitted in plain pasteurised or UHT drinking milk**; Lactovia's fresh-milk SKUs are therefore unaffected by this incident. Second, several traditional fermented-milk designations in Member State law restrict additive use; PLANT-DE-02's Bremen set-yogurt range is formulated conservatively for that reason.

## Purity criteria: Commission Regulation (EU) No 231/2012

Every incoming lot of pectin must meet the specifications in Regulation (EU) No 231/2012. A supplier's **certificate of conformity** is not acceptable at Lactovia goods-in unless it states, as a minimum:

- **Galacturonic acid content**: not less than **65%** on the ash-free and dried basis, calculated after washing with acid and alcohol.
- **Degree of esterification (DE)**: the classification driver. **DE ≥ 50%** is high-methoxyl (HM); **DE < 50%** is low-methoxyl (LM). ING-PEC-450 is specified at **DE 68–72%**.
- **Loss on drying**: not more than 12%.
- **Sulphur dioxide**: not more than **50 mg/kg** on the dried basis.
- **Methanol, ethanol and propan-2-ol**: not more than **1%** singly or in combination, on a solvent-free basis.
- **Acid-insoluble ash**: not more than 1%.
- **Heavy metals**: **lead ≤ 5 mg/kg, arsenic ≤ 3 mg/kg, cadmium ≤ 1 mg/kg, mercury ≤ 1 mg/kg**.
- For amidated pectin only: **degree of amidation not more than 25%** of the total carboxyl groups.

A certificate that omits DE or galacturonic acid is a rejection at goods-in, not a query. Lactovia QA retains one sealed sample per lot for 12 months beyond finished-product shelf life.

## Labelling: Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011

Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 governs the ingredient declaration. The decisive question is whether the **substance declared on pack** changes.

- **Same E440(i) HM grade, different supplier.** The declaration remains "pectin" or "stabiliser: pectin". **No label change is required**, provided the specification is identical, the allergen status is identical, the carrier/standardising agents (typically sucrose or dextrose, sometimes calcium salts) are the same, and no new QUID-relevant ingredient enters. This is the situation for a switch from SUP-EU-014 to SUP-EU-023 "Rheinland Hydrocolloids GmbH" on the same HM-450 grade.
- **E440(i) HM → E440(ii) amidated LM.** This is a **different additive**. The ingredient declaration changes, artwork must be reworked, and the change cannot ship on existing packaging. Standardising agents in LM systems frequently include **calcium chloride or calcium citrate**, which must also be declared.
- **Pectin → gelatin (ING-GEL-110, Bovine Gelatin 220 Bloom).** A full declaration change plus the **loss of vegetarian, Halal and Kosher claims**. Any on-pack "vegetarian", "suitable for vegetarians", V-Label, Halal or Kosher mark must be removed. Bovine gelatin is not an Annex II allergen, so no allergen emphasis is triggered, but the claim loss alone forces new artwork and a commercial decision.

**QUID** obligations are largely unaffected: pectin is a stabiliser, not a characterising ingredient, so a stabiliser swap does not normally disturb a quantitative fruit declaration. It does if the reformulation changes the fruit-preparation ratio to compensate for gel strength — a real risk when moving from HM to LM systems, which set with calcium rather than with sugar and acid.

## Organic production: Regulation (EU) 2018/848

This is the hardest constraint in the current incident. Annex V of Regulation (EU) 2018/848 lists the additives permitted in organic processed food. **E440(i) pectin (non-amidated) is permitted for products of plant and animal origin. E440(ii) amidated pectin is NOT permitted.**

Consequently, the EU-organic-certified SKUs in the **Lactovia Naturel** and **Lactovia Kids** ranges **cannot be reformulated onto amidated LM pectin at any price**. For those SKUs the only compliant routes are: an alternative source of non-amidated HM or LM pectin, a non-amidated hydrocolloid also listed in Annex V, or a temporary production stop.

Any new supplier feeding an organic line must additionally hold a valid **operator certificate** issued under Regulation (EU) 2018/848, and the material must be covered by a **Certificate of Inspection (COI)** raised in TRACES for consignments entering the EU from a third country.

This is why **SUP-APAC-009 "Nanjing Botanical Extracts" (Nanjing, China)** is problematic for organic lines specifically. Third-country organic material must arrive under the recognised-equivalence or compliance regime with a control-body-issued COI, and Lactovia's organic certifier requires documented mass-balance traceability from the raw citrus or apple pomace through to the finished pectin lot. Neither is currently in place, and the standing audit requirement plus a **60-day lead time** puts SUP-APAC-009 outside the incident window for organic SKUs. **SUP-LATAM-004 "Citrus Andina Ltda" (Curitiba, Brazil)** faces the same COI and equivalence workstream at a **55-day lead time**. **SUP-EU-023 (Krefeld, Germany)** is an EU operator and is the only alternate that can realistically be qualified for organic lines inside the window, at roughly an **18% price premium** on a **21-day lead time** and **40,000 kg/month** capacity.

## Artwork, over-stickers and national notification

Where a label change is unavoidable, plan **6–8 weeks** for print plate changes: 5–10 working days for artwork rework and regulatory sign-off, 10–15 for repro and plate manufacture, and the balance for the converter's print slot and delivery. Existing packaging stock must be written off or reworked.

A faster route exists. An **approved over-sticker derogation** — a compliant adhesive label applied over the affected panel, with the corrected ingredient list fully legible and not obscuring other mandatory particulars — can be authorised for a defined transition, in Lactovia's standing practice **valid for 90 days**. Over-stickers require QA sign-off, an application-quality check on line, and prior notification to the relevant national authority.

Notification is national, not EU-central. In France the competent authority is the **DGCCRF** (with DGAL for the veterinary/hygiene dimension of dairy establishments). In Germany, food law enforcement is a **Länder** competence — for PLANT-DE-02 that is the Bremen food-control authority, not a federal body. In Belgium it is the **FAVV/AFSCA**. Where an over-sticker or a temporary specification deviation is used on product placed in more than one Member State, each national authority is notified separately.

|---|---|---|---|
| Same E440(i) HM-450, new EU supplier (SUP-EU-023), identical spec | No | Yes, if supplier holds a valid organic operator certificate | 21 days (lead time) + 5 days goods-in qualification |

## Practical sequence for the 2026-09-03 incident

1. Segregate the 14 affected SKUs into **organic-certified** and **conventional** sets. Organic SKUs are constrained to non-amidated pectin from a certified operator; SUP-EU-023 is the only viable path inside the window.
2. For conventional SKUs, qualify SUP-EU-023 on the identical E440(i) HM grade first — it requires **no artwork change** and is therefore the fastest compliant substitution.
3. Open the audit and COI workstream for SUP-LATAM-004 and SUP-APAC-009 immediately as a second wave; neither can serve PLANT-FR-01's 12-day depletion horizon.
4. Treat any amidated-LM or gelatin reformulation as an artwork project with a 6–8 week critical path, and evaluate the 90-day over-sticker derogation with DGCCRF, the Bremen authority and FAVV/AFSCA in parallel.
5. Verify every incoming certificate of conformity against the Regulation (EU) No 231/2012 parameters above before releasing any substitute lot to production.
